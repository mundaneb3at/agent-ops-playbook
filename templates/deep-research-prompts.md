# Outside-advisor deep-research prompts — ready to paste

**What this is:** prompt templates for a manual, human-driven deep-research product (the kind you
open in a browser, sign in, and click "run" — not an API call). This is the "outside-advisor" role
described in [README.md](README.md): a human step, deliberately, because a subscription-gated
research tool generally can't be automated the way an API can.

**Why manual, stated plainly:** don't oversell this as automatable. It's a button you press when a
topic needs real depth — the deliberate cost is that a human has to be there to press it and skim
the result before trusting it.

**How to use:** open your chosen deep-research tool, paste a template below, fill in the `<...>`
blanks, and run it. Skim its research plan before approving it if the tool shows you one; demand
real sources; treat every number it returns as unverified until you actually click through to the
source yourself.

---

## 1 — Tool / tech landscape ("what's the current best X for Y")

> Research the current (as of `<month year>`) landscape of **`<tool category>`** for my use case:
> **`<one line — your real constraints: free/self-hostable, platform, budget, hardware, etc.>`**.
> For each serious option give: what it does, maturity/maintenance status, cost (free tier vs
> paid), the specific trade-off vs its closest alternative, and a primary source (official docs or
> repo — not a listicle). End with a ranked shortlist for MY constraints and an honest "what I
> still couldn't confirm." Flag anything that changed in the last 3 months. Do not recommend paid
> options unless there is no free/self-hostable equivalent.

## 2 — Evidence deep-dive ("what does the research actually say about X")

> Find the strongest *empirical* evidence on **`<claim/topic>`**. Prioritize peer-reviewed studies
> and systematic reviews over blog posts. For each key source: the finding, sample/method, effect
> size if given, and the limitation that most weakens it. Map where credible sources *disagree*
> and why. Separate "well-supported" from "promising but thin" from "hype." Give me real citations
> (title + venue + year + link) I can verify myself. End with "what a skeptic would say."

## 3 — Concept mastery (study / learning a topic in depth)

> I'm studying **`<subject + specific topic>`**. Build me a deep explainer: the core principle from
> first principles, the key formulas/relationships and exactly when each applies, the most common
> mistake people make with this, and 2 worked examples of increasing difficulty with full steps.
> Ground it in standard reference treatment for the field. Flag any point where conventions differ
> between sources. Keep it rigorous — show the derivation, don't just state results.

## 4 — Decision / cost comparison ("should I pick A or B, and what does it really cost")

> Compare **`<option A>`** vs **`<option B>`** for **`<my situation>`**. Build a side-by-side on the
> axes that actually matter to me: real all-in cost (including hidden/recurring), free-tier limits,
> lock-in, learning curve, and how each fails. Surface the non-obvious gotcha for each (the thing
> you only learn after committing). Cite primary sources for any pricing or limit claim — pricing
> pages change, so date them. End with a recommendation FOR my constraints plus the one condition
> that would flip it.
